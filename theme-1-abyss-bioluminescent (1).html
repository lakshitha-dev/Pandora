The retrieved evidence matches a Stage-2 algal proliferation signature. Protocol requires water sampling and a shellfish-harvest suspension before the next tidal cycle.

What the evidence indicates

Rust-orange discolouration in the eastern bay is most consistent with a dinoflagellate bloom triggered by nutrient runoff. Bay-7 monitoring recorded nitrate at 4.8 mg/L — 3.2× the seasonal baseline — with dissolved oxygen falling to 4.1 mg/L over the same window 1. Field notes describe an identical colour shift preceding the Kelutral inlet event two seasons ago 2.

Departure of ilu from the shallows is a documented early avoidance response to hypoxia, and is treated in the protocol as a leading indicator rather than an incidental observation 3.

Species at elevated risk

  • Reef-brooding corals (3 spp.) — bleaching threshold is crossed below 5.0 mg/L dissolved oxygen 1
  • Filter-feeding shellfish beds — bioaccumulate the toxin; primary human-exposure pathway 3
  • Juvenile ilu nurseries — the eastern shallows are the only recorded nursery in this range 4

Recommended immediate actions

  1. Take water samples at 3 depths across 5 transects; log nitrate, DO, and cell counts 3
  2. Suspend shellfish harvesting bay-wide until two consecutive clear counts 3
  3. Notify the Tsahìk council — oral records hold the historical bloom cycle for this bay 2
  4. Trace upstream runoff sources along the northern watershed 1

Where the evidence is thin

No document in the current knowledge base specifies a toxin-clearance time for shellfish beds after a bloom subsides. Treat the harvest suspension as open-ended until a clearance study is added to the corpus.
